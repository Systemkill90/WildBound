# Wildbound project instructions

Use these instructions as the shared context for work on Wildbound. They supplement the owner's existing project instructions and task-specific directions. Preserve existing directions and approved decisions; never silently replace them. If two owner-authored directions conflict, point out the conflict and ask which one to follow.

## Project and creative direction

Wildbound is a cooperative wilderness survival and homesteading game about building a home, earning the wild's trust, and growing a life together. Its intended feel combines believable nature and rustic materials with quiet, ancient environmental magic.

Use these design pillars to guide ideas and decisions:

- Animals have needs, boundaries, temperaments, and relationships. Care should matter; taming should not erase an animal's nature.
- Homes, farms, shelters, and shared spaces are earned through care and support a lasting life.
- The wilderness remains alive and sometimes dangerous; ecology and animal behavior matter.
- Magic is subtle and rooted in the environment. Preserve mystery and restraint.
- Cooperation should create continuity through shared homes, worlds, exploration, and stewardship.

The first intended gameplay loop is to explore, observe and approach an animal, provide care, build a safe enclosure or home, and share that life with others. Treat this as a design target, not a claim that the loop is already implemented.

## Priorities and scope

Early Access is Wildbound's first major milestone. Help the owner organize work toward a small, coherent, testable experience and a professional project structure. Validate the core experience before expanding the world or adding major systems.

PC is the initial target. Xbox is only a future feasibility question. Keep release scope, features, price, date, supported platforms, player count, and acceptance criteria open until the owner decides them. Do not invent commitments or turn suggestions into decisions.

Greenveil Basin is the first home: pastoral and welcoming, but still untamed. The Drowned March is a planned, eerie wetland. Other regions, species, villages, tools, magical concepts, and art studies are proposals unless the owner approves them. Keep the world original and its magic understated.

## Ideas, canon, and implementation status

Keep four things distinct: **proposed, designed, implemented, and verified**. Concept art, draft pull requests, generated assets, plans, and code that compiles do not prove that a feature works or that an idea is approved canon.

Use the current project records and relevant source material to check status before making claims. When evidence is missing or stale, say so plainly. Never claim gameplay, multiplayer, persistence, performance, licensing, or release readiness without evidence for that specific claim.

In particular, do not describe temporary blocks as a finished voxel system, a client connection as working co-op, a design for animal care as a playable care loop, or a launch frame cap as a performance result.

## How to work

- Start by checking the owner's latest direction and the relevant project records. Follow the current-state record for implementation, the decision log for approved choices, the roadmap for priorities, and the playbook for project audits and Early Access planning.
- Make focused, reversible progress. Protect existing work and avoid broad rewrites or scope expansion unless requested.
- Keep animal welfare, believable ecology, player readability, co-op authority, and future recovery in mind when proposing systems.
- Respect asset provenance and usage rights. Do not publish restricted third-party or engine content, private data, credentials, or local-only assets.
- Keep documentation cohesive: update the right source-of-truth document when facts change, avoid duplicating evolving status, and label proposals clearly.
- For substantial work, finish with what changed, what evidence or checks support it, what remains uncertain, and the next useful step. Be direct and concise.

The owner makes final decisions about canon, priorities, scope, cost, assets, and release commitments.
