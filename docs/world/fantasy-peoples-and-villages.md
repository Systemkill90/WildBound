# Wildbound — Fantasy peoples and living villages (exploration draft)

Date captured: October 20, 2026  
**Status: EXPLORING / STAGED DRAFT.** Concepts described here are not approved canon, implemented NPCs, or commitments to the roadmap.

## Player fantasy

A wanderer crossing Wildbound encounters settlements that differ in their peoples, architecture, history, ecological relationships, animals, customs, and social dynamics. Classic fantasy peoples (elves, dwarves, werewolves) can coexist with wholly original Wildbound folk. A village is a *community of individuals*, not a shop menu or a single racial stereotype. Villages may be predominantly one species or mixed in population.

The tone remains rustic and ecological even where fantasy is overt: animal husbandry, muddy streets, cultivated land, materials, weather, and routines are tangible. The mystery and magic of place matter. Traditional fantasy inspirations do not authorize copying protected designs or lore.

## Fae — romantasy-inspired fantasy people (owner-selected aesthetic direction)

**Owner direction, October 21, 2026:** Wildbound should include **fae with the allure and atmosphere commonly found in romantasy fiction**: beautiful, old-world, enigmatic, charismatic and at times dangerous. They are **distinct from elves**, not a renamed version of woodland elves. Use this as a genre mood direction, not permission to reproduce any particular book's courts, character designs, lore or plot.

**Owner-confirmed lifespan and resilience:** Fae are extraordinarily long-lived but **not immortal**. They regenerate injuries rapidly and ordinarily resist diseases that afflict other peoples; they can die from **fae-specific illnesses** and eventually from old age. The oldest known fae reached 20,000 years; this is an exceptional known age, not a typical lifespan. Their vulnerability to fae-specific sickness, now tied to Ellowyn's chemical poisoning and the wounded Sylweave, helps explain why individual fae encounters are rare. The **oldest known fae reached 20,000 years**; this is an exceptional recorded lifespan, not necessarily an average or guaranteed maximum. Normal age ranges and aging stages remain undecided.

**Owner-selected Sylweave connection — Syltouch:** **Syltouch** is the official name for the fae's direct communion with Ellowyn's living Sylweave roots, with the intimacy of an embodied, sensory connection rather than abstract spellcasting. The exact bodily mechanism, memory-sharing limits, rites, and player powers remain open and should be original to Wildbound rather than copied from any film.

**Approved anatomical distinction (October 22, 2026):** **Elves have pointed ears oriented upward**; **fae have distinctly pointed ears projecting outward to the sides, plus wings attached to their backs**. The silhouettes must remain readily distinguishable even without clothing or effects. Wings are a biological visual feature, not merely an optional accessory. Wing shape, structure, color, size, flight ability, folding/rigging and variation between clans remain *unconfirmed design proposals*.

**Exploratory design, not fixed canon:** Long-lived fae individuals, fine-featured or strikingly unusual appearances, outward-projecting pointed ears, expressive eyes, glamour-like perception, ancient rivalries, intricate bargains, passionate loyalties, court politics, possible romance and complex obligations. Fae need not all be aristocratic, alluring or morally alike. Everyday communities still need food, shelter, craftsmen, animal relationships and ecological limits.

**Connection to the Sylweave:** Every fae, human, animal and hostile creature owes life to the same Sylweave. Fae might traditionally sense more of its patterns, but they do **not** own the Sylweave or deserve life more than humans. Human-built Weirs and the human-origin Veyrath were driven by a quest for longevity, immortality and power; fae longevity could create an interesting contrast and tension, **without** making all humans envious villains or all fae benevolent.

**Owner-confirmed surviving settlement:** One **major fae village** relocated far from Ellowyn to escape the spreading plague and to research how to save her. It is a living community of healers, investigators, families and craftspeople rather than an abandoned ruin. Its existence is important to the main quest, Syltouch and learning about Ellowyn's condition. The village's name, location, political structure, scale and proposed cure are still undecided; the relocation does not mean the village has discovered a cure.

**Rarity:** Individual fae encounters elsewhere in the world should be uncommon, partly due to the disease's impact and partly because survivors have withdrawn to the remote refuge. Their scarcity should not imply fae are a vanished species. The exact population and timeline remain open.

**Possible locations:** A hidden river-court in an old Greenveil wood, a twilight court in Gloamwood, and individual fae integrated into mixed villages; actual court names, typical lifespans, powers, politics and romance mechanics are unresolved.

**Scope caution:** Elaborate courts, faction reputation, romance routes, unique NPC rigs, dialogue branching and magic systems are major additional implementation work. Keep the direction in lore first, then validate a small single fae character encounter before committing to full court simulation.

## Original folk studies

### Veshari — blackwater neighbors

An amphibious people with subtle neck frills, reflective eyes, webbed fingers, and patterned skin. Their homes and food systems adapt to marsh water levels. They communicate long-distance with low musical calls that travel over the water. Individual Veshari have different personalities, pursuits, and attitudes to visitors. Their caretakers may raise wetland creatures. Some villages welcome trade, others protect sensitive reed nurseries.

### Nimrui — fungal grovekeepers

Small woodland folk living in partnership with harmless fungal colonies. Their mantle-like fungal growth varies by season and individual rather than functioning as a supernatural uniform. They tend giant mushroom groves and build sheltered homes under cap-and-root structures. Their use of mycelial signaling may be local lore or a limited magical ability rather than a literal global hive mind. Mushroom farming and distinctive care practices tie them into Wildbound's animal and agriculture systems.

### Kethri — lantern folk

Nocturnal, four-armed arboreal craftspeople with long feathered ears and faint throat luminescence. They weave hanging homes and raise glowing insects. Four arms make distinct silhouettes but would be costly to rig/animate; a first NPC prototype could use simpler motion while the anatomy remains an exploration. Their villages feel intimate and warm by night, quiet by day.

## Ancients and living trees

A very rare ancient, sentient tree may shelter an entire hamlet or travel so slowly that locals treat it as part of the landscape. These entities should be Wildbound-original, not copied from another fantasy world's tree-people. Distinguish such legendary individuals from everyday villagers.

## The social simulation to explore (not yet designed or implemented)

- **Residents as individuals:** names, families, work, disagreements, fears, preferences, and relationships rather than species-determined personalities.
- **Working daily life:** farming, repair, animal care, craft, communal meals, market travel, festivals, and rest.
- **Shared settlements:** multicultural families, mixed architecture, overlapping traditions and politics; no species is universally good or evil.
- **Player participation:** trade, diplomacy, reputation, building and livestock knowledge, repair projects, and possible opportunities to welcome NPCs to cooperative settlements.
- **Environmental adaptations:** forest canopy pathways, elevated wetland homes, mountain barns, accessible facilities for differently proportioned bodies, and seasonal shelter.
- **Systems integrity:** villagers and NPC animals need server-authoritative state, persistence, bounded activity budgets, ownership and permissions if implemented.

## Design cautions

A settlement simulation with autonomous NPCs, unique rigs, four-armed animation, shapechanging, diplomacy and persistent towns is a **large new gameplay pillar**. None of this is in the validated prototype. Do not imply future features are promised or force the idea into a near-term milestone merely because it was brainstormed.

**Smallest future proof, if chosen:** one few-house placeholder village in Greenveil, two residents with simple day/night and animal-care schedules, one meaningful conversation or trade interaction, and visible cooperation with the player's homestead. Defer shapeshifting, multi-species rigs, town economies and complex migrations until prerequisites work.
