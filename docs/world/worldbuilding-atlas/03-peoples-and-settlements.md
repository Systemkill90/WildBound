# 03 | Peoples, water villages and social geography
**Everything beyond the approved Fae direction is exploratory.**

## Cultural differentiation framework
A people is not a costume. Every culture needs (a) economic role, (b) habitat adaptation, (c) home architecture, (d) relationship to fauna, (e) relationship to contamination, and (f) how players build trust. Avoid mandatory ethnic morality or universal hostility.

| Working society | Environment and shelter proposal | Distinct daily life | Potential player encounter | Approval |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rural humans | Timber cottages, mills, fieldstone yards | Farming, livestock, trade, disagreement over the Veyrath | Repair bridge, trade seed | Candidate settlement examples |
| Dwarven-inspired mountain folk | Terraced stone, windbreaks, roofed forges | Quarrying, metalwork, goat herding | Find safe mine supports | Exploring |
| Woodland cultures | Elevated timber corridors, moss stone, forest clearings | Sylvan forestry and foraging | Negotiate sustainable cutting | Exploring |
| Shapeshifter / werewolf-associated people | Deep woods and edge hamlets, wide common rooms | Kinship and seasonal rituals | Protect livestock *and* community dignity | Exploring |
| **Veshari / Nimrui / Kethri** | To be distinguished, not assigned stereotypes | Original folk names only | Research genuinely original social roles | Exploring |
| **Brinefolk** | Stilt docks, ropes, bell lines | Nets, reeds, tide/fog routes | Hear a work shanty while trading | Exploring |
| **Tidemarked / merfolk** | Shallow lagoons to drowned halls | Waterborne remembrance and communal architecture | Recover a stone inscription | Exploring |
| **Luresingers / sirens** | Fog channels, shoals and offshore ruins | Haunting duet traditions | Discern warning from invitation | Exploring |

## Settlement size tiers (proposal)
- **Outpost:** 3–8 structures, 1–2 service hooks; first feasible NPC site.
- **Hamlet:** 8–25 structures, one central communal routine.
- **Village:** 25–75 structures, craft areas, local governance and animal shelters.
- **Regional center:** concept-only until navigation, simulation and streaming budgets exist.

Numbers describe **design intent**, not tested world limits.

## Three aquatic sound concepts
- *Low the Lantern* — Brinefolk working shanty; intimate communal call/response; mournful but warm.
- *Stone Below the Water* — Tidemarked ancestral underwater memorial hymn.
- *The Third Bell* — Luresinger siren duet; **deep feminine harmonies and clear, eerily angelic male voice** (owner-selected sound direction).
Original draft lyrics and generation prompts live in the staged [song sheets](../../audio/song-generator-sheets/README.md) on the earlier idea-board PR; final tracks, rights, singer identities and in-game audio are not settled.

## Water-village gameplay constraints
Swimming and water navigation, camera, buoyancy, AI pathing, underwater readability and persistence are heavy dependencies. **First test:** one stilt dock with one NPC, one dry walkable path and one ambient singing sound cue. Do not promise fully underwater villages until traversal and server replication work.

## Quest framing
Use errands that reveal habits, trade and tensions. Help a Brinefolk community mend a warning bell; discover a riverfolk's missing route marker; learn why a village locks its night gates. No culture should exist only to deliver a combat quest.
