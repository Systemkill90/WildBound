## Story spine

In the oldest grove of Wildbound stands **Ellowyn**, a sentient weeping willow larger and older than any known tree. Her roots extend beneath forests, villages, mountains and the drowned lowlands. A subtle living current moves through them, nourishing the world's rhythms of growth, migration, decay and renewal.

Ancient engineers discovered that this current could be directed. Their **Weirs** originally sheltered communities from failed harvests, violent floods and disease, but attempts to hold conditions permanently stable began disrupting nature's change and recovery. In the collapse that followed, much of the lowlands became the **Drowned March**. The devices were abandoned but not completely destroyed.

A new faction has found the Weirs and learned how to use them as extraction lines. For wealth, political control and unnaturally prolonged life, its leaders now **mine the current directly from Ellowyn's living roots**, refining it into an energy-rich substance provisionally called **Heartglass**.

The early symptoms look like inexplicable local changes: animals lose their migration paths, an old orchard blossoms without producing viable seeds, reeds bloom out of season, a great fungal grove ceases to regenerate, and Ellowyn's most distant roots wither. An ordinary homesteader gradually realizes the world's failing rhythms are symptoms of someone hurting an individual living being.

**The central quest:** Find Ellowyn, discover what is being done to her, expose and stop the extraction network, and repair the damage before it becomes irreversible—without destroying towns and habitats that ancient Weirs still protect.

## Ellowyn as a character, not a prop

Ellowyn is patient, thoughtful, sometimes dryly funny, and ancient without being infallible. She can be stubborn, afraid, generous and exhausted. She has protected living creatures and settlements for ages and made difficult choices of her own. Her capacity to choose matters to the ending: she is not a magical machine for players to repair without consent.

She may speak in the rustle of boughs, a low resonant voice in the trunk, water reflections or dreams. Her voice should not be an imitation of any existing fantasy tree character.

Possible original line:  
> “I once knew the footsteps of every creature who crossed my shade. Now whole valleys fall silent beneath me.”

Players could first care for a small creature sleeping at the base of one of her remote roots without understanding what the root belongs to. Their relationship with Ellowyn grows through actions, not an instant proclamation that they are the chosen savior.

## The adversary: The Hollow Crown (working faction name)

A coalition of powerful patrons, excavators, engineers and hired protectors. The leadership knowingly redirects Ellowyn's living essence into mines and underground conduits, refining **Heartglass** to extend life, run extraordinary machinery and command dependency.

The Hollow Crown can contain people with conflicting motives. Some workers believe they are building flood defenses or producing life-saving medicine. One settlement may legitimately rely on its Heartglass-powered protection. No culture or fantasy species is inherently villainous.

A possible principal antagonist, **the Gilded Warden** (working title), once wanted to save people from suffering but now believes his continued rule and engineered stability justify Ellowyn's slow death. A separate ancient custodian from the old story, **the Keeper Below**, may have protective instructions and become an obstacle, reluctant ally or moral witness rather than a second generic evil overlord.

### Why simple destruction is dangerous

Not every Weir is malicious. Some hold back floodwaters or stabilize an inhabited cliff. Destroying the entire network could unleash catastrophes, including another drowning of the March. The antagonist exploits this genuine risk to claim that Ellowyn's suffering is necessary. The players need more than a sword or explosives: they must find a way to isolate extraction while maintaining essential safety functions long enough to restore local habitats.

## Story movements (narrative beats, not development milestones)

### I — A Root Beneath the Meadow

Players arrive and build a modest Greenveil home, care for animals and encounter familiar signs of the unfamiliar: a spring running bitter, a gentle grazer refusing a meadow, a buried root that seems to withdraw from the touch of metal. Ellowyn speaks through a small branch or dream, too weak to reveal everything.

The first quest offers a tangible rescue: shut off one hidden feeder conduit near a vulnerable habitat, restoring a stream and allowing a small animal family to return. This is the player's first proof that direct care can matter.

### II — Voices of the Old World

Elven orchard-tenders describe trees that bloom without seed. Dwarven settlements know the under-mountain machinery. Werewolf villagers track animal behavior and seasons. Nimrui fungal keepers witness disrupted decomposition. Some villagers embrace Heartglass, others fear it.

The player follows scattered Weir sites, negotiates with people who disagree, gathers practical knowledge and earns help. Allies bring different solutions; players should not need to befriend every civilization or grind every farming system to complete the story.

### III — The Drowned Roots

In the Drowned March, water peoples preserve fragments of the old disaster through charts, ruins, songs and oral tradition. One old shanty may refer to a drowned approach beneath an ancient bell, but the clue also exists through visual inscriptions for accessibility.

A revelation: the old engineers knew they were drawing on a **living being**. Some struggled to protect Ellowyn; others disguised the truth. The Hollow Crown has revived and expanded the worst part of the old system. The deeper they drill, the more Ellowyn can feel but cannot reach her own roots.

### IV — Beneath Ellowyn's Heartwood

The Hollow Crown activates the **Great Siphon** beneath a chamber formed by living roots and old stone architecture. A final choice confronts the player: attack the miners recklessly and risk the collapse of protective infrastructure, or work with allies to sever extraction and rebuild distributed safeguards.

The finale can involve exploration, structural puzzles, combat with armed defenders/constructs if combat is later chosen, animal assistance, village support and a dramatic confrontation with a principal antagonist. No real gameplay system is promised in this concept.

**Ellowyn actively participates** in her own rescue. When the siphon weakens, she can choose to redirect her returning current, protecting vulnerable settlements while trusting living communities to adapt rather than holding the world unchanged forever.

## What actually counts as beating the game?

The **Rescue of Ellowyn** is a clear final-story victory, not an endlessly vague ecological meter. Proposed criteria:

1. Establish a foothold/home and discover the first suffering root.
2. Learn who is extracting Ellowyn's current and why; gather enough evidence and allies to confront them.
3. Reach the buried extraction network through regional mysteries and inhabited landscapes.
4. Disable the Great Siphon and stop the Hollow Crown's leadership or secure its surrender/defeat.
5. Save Ellowyn and stabilize necessary Weir protections, allowing the world's interrupted cycles to resume.

The finale has a definitive climactic scene, title/credits, visible recovery of Ellowyn and in-world recognition. The persistent co-op world continues after credits; the player's homes, animals, settlements and saved relationships survive. No mandatory wipe, sacrifice of owned animals, or arbitrary destruction of the Drowned March.

### Epilogue candidate

As the Great Siphon falls silent, deep fissures close along the living root. In scattered settlements, water rises and then finds its natural course. An ancient grove begins producing seedlings. Ellowyn's willow boughs sway without wind. The sirens sing an unfamiliar final harmony. For the first time in an age, the great tree can feel distant rain traveling through her roots.

Ellowyn does not become an invulnerable source of infinite magic. She lives, slowly recovers, and continues to need time, ecosystem balance and respectful protection. The rescued world is not perfectly safe—but it can now heal.

## Open creative decisions

- Final name for the current: **Vaelith, Eversong, Sylweave, Aelthryn, Firstsong, Evervein, or something newly invented?**
- Is Ellowyn discovered early and visited often, or revealed only after clues point to her?
- Is the main foe a person with a name/face and a boss-like final encounter, an industrial faction, or both?
- Should the protagonist be able to preserve beneficial Weirs through difficult negotiations and physical reconstruction rather than merely shutting them all down?
- Should the main story have one canonical rescue ending or several variations in how the antagonist and Weirs are handled?

These are open story decisions; do not record them as canon without owner approval.
