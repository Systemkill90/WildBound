# Wildbound — Enemy faction concepts and creature taxonomy

**Date:** October 21, 2026. **Stage:** The faction name **The Veyrath** is owner-approved; ranks, characters, combat mechanics, assets and dialogue remain exploratory and unimplemented.

[Ellowyn and the Sylweave](ellowyn-and-the-sylweave.md) · [Quests](quest-framework.md) · [Fantasy villages](fantasy-peoples-and-villages.md) · [Biomes](biomes.md) · [Idea board](../project/IDEA-BOARD.md)

## Fixed lore constraints from the owner

Ellowyn is the oldest sentient willow; her roots carry the **Sylweave**, a force that creates and connects **every living thing, including evil beings**. A faction seeking youth, power and immortality uses old extraction machinery and a **specific poisonous chemical**, which blights Ellowyn. The chemical contaminates harvested Sylweave, so the faction repeatedly drills deeper seeking a pure batch; the repetition poisons Ellowyn more. The faction itself becomes sick and some of its members transform into monsters. Ellowyn spends what strength she has left to gift the players with Sylweave potential; they must survive, care for animals, learn the world, train and grow able to save her. Bonded animals can help fight the faction, with species-appropriate limits. The faction is named **The Veyrath** by the owner.

| **Gilded Veil** | A clandestine network of wealthy patrons and alchemists with a ceremonial aesthetic |
| **Ashen Covenant** | A doctrinal brotherhood treating mortality as an illness to eliminate |

Historical notes mention **Hollow Crown**, also a previous unapproved candidate. **The Veyrath** is the official faction name; Gilded Veil, Ashen Covenant and Hollow Crown remain rejected or superseded name suggestions.

## Origin and downfall — proposed

The organization started among physicians, engineers, alchemists, and scholars genuinely hoping to heal suffering. A forgotten Weir demonstrated that Sylweave extraction could temporarily rejuvenate people. Wealthy sponsors financed refinements. A reagent (previously proposed working name **Nhalis**, still unapproved) enabled collection, transport and preservation of the energy (previously proposed product **Heartglass**, also unapproved).

Early miracles masked a contradiction: the chemical enters Ellowyn's roots through the extraction machinery and spoils the current itself. The harvests grow ever more contaminated and the treated bodies deteriorate. Instead of recognizing that the extraction process causes the plague, leaders assume that some untouched Sylweave reservoir remains beyond the poisoned zone. They drill longer, deeper, and wider, creating a closed loop of more contamination, worse treatments, progressively sicker researchers and an ever-weaker Ellowyn.

Neither the Sylweave nor the faction's member species is intrinsically evil. Individual members can be opportunists, believers, deceived researchers, desperate patients, coerced workers or deliberate perpetrators. Living creatures who oppose Ellowyn still derive life from the same Sylweave.

## Public face and visual design

Before players know its practices, the faction appears as a respectable medical, engineering and public-safety association. It funds clinics, flood defenses, research caravans, and construction. Buildings are immaculate where visitors enter, but industrial spaces below contain root-bore clamps, stained filters, pale crystalline waste and increasingly damaged stonework.

Distinctive materials: dark weathered steel, tarnished brass, pale enamel, bone-white linen, velvet insignia and branching patterns that imitate Ellowyn's roots while literally binding and constricting them. Avoid obvious skull-and-spike villain armor from the start. A progression from polished ceremonial gear to improvised protective wrappings and deforming armor conveys internal collapse.

Proposed emblem: a symmetrical crown of five rootlike prongs around a sealed glass vessel. Not yet a canon asset.

## Enemy ranks (all working terms)

| Role | Identity | Encounter possibility |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Seekers** | Prospectors, recruiters, spies and mapkeepers | Early tracking, scouting, intercepted letters |
| **Wardens** | Paid security and trained guards | Patrol fights, protecting convoys, camp defense |
| **Artificers** | Reagent mixers and Weir engineers | Stop injector lines, disable pumps, rescue workers |
| **Vessels** | Volunteers/patrons receiving repeated longevity treatments | Enhanced adversaries with escalating side effects |
| **Hollowed** | People profoundly altered by exposure | Dangerous but sometimes frightened, aware or recoverable individuals |
| **Inner Concord** | Secretive senior patrons and research directors | Moral choices, named antagonists, major encounters |

These are *encounter concepts*, not a promise of specific combat mechanics. Distinguish employees who must be stopped from noncombatants and those needing rescue; avoid portraying illness or disfigurement as an automatic moral failure.

## Central antagonist candidate — Lord Vaedryn

Former healer whose obsession with preventing the death of a loved one becomes fear of mortality. He initially saved people, then refused to accept that the extraction machines themselves make a clean batch impossible. Publicly gracious and austere; privately his elaborate armor and clothing conceal increasing chemical injury. He sees the continuing sacrifice of Ellowyn as a regrettable necessity for his life's work.

Sample original dialogue (not final):

> “Death is not sacred. It is merely the oldest disease, and I will not bow before it.”

This offers emotional motives, but does not excuse continued exploitation. His exact species, fate and role as final opponent remain undecided. A 'Keeper Below' from an earlier story draft could survive as a separate old infrastructure custodian, not a second redundant villain.

## Body-change progression — proposed stages, not a deterministic illness analogy

1. **False renewal:** Apparent youth or vigor, unnatural sleeplessness, subtle pale lines under the skin.
2. **Blighting:** Increasing dependence, disturbed voice, visible crystalline lesions, asymmetric extremities, difficulty regulating emotions.
3. **Hollowing:** Radical alterations in silhouette, painful warped tissue and unstable abilities, sometimes monstrous behavior and strength.
4. **Rare deviation:** A heavily exposed leader may retain high intelligence and planning while physically transformed, leading to a climactic boss candidate.

Factions members do not all become monsters; some fight, some defect, some are coerced, and some can be helped. No real-world chemical formula or procedural poisoning technique is proposed.

## Battle ecosystem and animal-companion roles

The player's earned animal bonds should matter without reducing wildlife to equipment. Possible roles: pack predators defend against pursuers; flying creatures spot patrols or carry warning; trained large grazers shield allies and push barricades; burrowers find hidden conduits; mythical wildlife senses Sylweave blight; large fantasy saurians provide rare, high-care defensive capacity. Companion stress, training, injury, retreat and noncombat support remain necessary. No main-story victory should require sacrificing a pet.

Enemy NPCs can also possess relationships with animals. Some might abuse or force creatures; others genuinely care for them. Questlines can reveal faction divisions rather than treating all adversaries alike.

## Where the faction appears

- **Greenveil:** Polite emissary, medical wagon, survey stakes near an ill creek, then a small concealed injection site.
- **Stonewake heights:** Fortified mine, displaced roots and old Weir machinery; an engineer asks for help saving a threatened village.
- **Drowned March:** Chemical settling pools, half-submerged filters, haunted barracks and failed experiments; fragments of songs identify former routes.
- **Other villages:** Political dependence on restorative product, trade disputes, clinics, whistleblowers, mercenary patrols.

## Five quests to test the arc

1. **The Gentle Physician** — First encounter with a benevolent-seeming faction clinic; a villager's real recovery complicates the choice.
2. **Marks in the Creek** — Follow animal behavior and contaminated water toward a feeder pipe.
3. **A Still-Young Face** — Meet a treatment recipient who appears healthy but fears emerging symptoms; investigate research logs.
4. **The Deepest Seam** — Stop a drilling project threatening inhabited land; recruit local craft expertise.
5. **The Great Siphon** — With allied villagers and trained animal companions, halt extraction and chemical release without indiscriminately destroying protective Weirs.

Reward directions: artisan plans, salvage rights, animal-care equipment, building sets, historical truths, clothing, faction disguises, new travel routes and restoration options. Do not make all content a loot ladder.

## Bestiary handoff — taxonomy proposal

Start the world bestiary with separate categories: **ordinary wildlife**, **farm animals**, **mythical wildlife**, **fantasy saurians**, **bondable working and guardian animals**, **sentient peoples**, **contaminated creatures**, and **faction members/transformed individuals**. Avoid placing intelligent cultures in a monster-only category. Each entry should record species and habitat, temperament, needs, behavior, social relationships, danger, taming/rehabilitation possibility, companion utility, visual reference, and legal/asset status.

**First manageable validation later:** a faction Seeker, one small chemical-leak site, a wild animal reacting to blight, a single observation objective and a protective companion behavior. The main Unreal prototype has not implemented any of this. The repository currently documents a compiled Greenveil test environment, but quest combat, animal allies, complex NPCs, enemy factions, dedicated server behaviors and persistence are not verified.
