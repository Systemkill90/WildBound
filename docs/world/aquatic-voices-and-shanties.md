# Wildbound — Aquatic voices, slow shanties and songbook

Captured October 21, 2026 · **EXPLORATORY AUDIO DIRECTION / DRAFT LYRICS**  
[Idea board](../project/IDEA-BOARD.md) · [Fantasy villages](fantasy-peoples-and-villages.md) · [Drowned March](biomes.md)

## Creative brief

Aquatic NPCs in Wildbound should sing **slow, ethereal, creepy and enchanting shanties** in-world. Their songs are not generic soundtrack: they are living traditions that travel over fog, water and village docks. Heard before a settlement is visible, a distant chorus can be both a welcome and a warning. The world remains rustic, ecological and quietly magical rather than theatrical fantasy.

These are original, newly authored lyric drafts. Titles, melodies, vocal designs, peoples' names and gameplay hooks are proposals, not approved canon or implemented audio. Recorded singers, sample licenses, song approvals and game deployment are not yet established.

### Common sonic vocabulary

- **Singing first, instruments second**: aged, imperfect humanlike breath, pauses, calls and varied harmonies. No jaunty tavern-pirate arrangements or glossy epic orchestra.
- **Pacing**: mostly 38–56 quarter-note beats per minute or free-time phrases; breathing and rowing may determine pulse more than a rigid click.
- **Materials**: creaking boat timber, rope tension, distant fog-bells, wood knocks, shell chimes, reed flute, frame drum, water drops, underwater resonance.
- **Mood**: beautiful in a way that makes players listen, then uneasy as they wonder what exactly is being sung.
- **Distinctiveness**: Brinefolk songs are communal and tactile; merfolk songs are intimate and reverent; siren songs are impossible to place and may seem familiar for no clear reason.
- **Language**: begin with understandable English lyrics; optionally later design in-world dialect syllables with cultural consistency and accessibility captions. Avoid randomized nonsensical chant presented as a complete language.

## Song 01 — BRINEFOLK: “Low the Lantern”

**Use:** Evening net-mending, slow boats coming home, village chorus that first leads the player to a stilt settlement.  
**Draft musical direction:** 52 BPM, slow rolling 6/8, D Dorian; one weathered voice starts, 2–4 residents answer in loosely aligned harmony. Wood taps at each oar pull, weak rope creak and drifting marsh wash. Dry close vocals on docks; softened and distant across water.

**Verse I**
> Pull the rope and mend the seam,  
> Wake the oars from willow dream.  
> Count the poles along the shore,  
> One for those who row no more.

**Chorus**
> Low the lantern, slow the oar,  
> Let the dark go first ashore.  
> Keep the hearth where reed roots grow,  
> And the water knows, the water knows.

**Verse II**
> Silt on hands and salt on skin,  
> Bring the night's small harvest in.  
> If the fog takes half the land,  
> Follow bells, not beckoning hands.

**Chorus**
> Low the lantern, slow the oar,  
> Let the dark go first ashore.  
> Keep the hearth where reed roots grow,  
> And the water knows, the water knows.

**Bridge**
> When the black tide climbs the stair,  
> Leave an empty place and chair.  
> Sing no names against the foam;  
> The missing know the way back home.

**Final refrain (half-voice, almost whispered)**
> Low the lantern, slow the oar...  
> The water knows. The water knows.

**Performance direction:** Not sad all the way through: warm neighborly undertones make the dark lyric land harder. Slightly late backing voices, worn consonants and audible air; never mechanically synchronized.

## Song 02 — MERFOLK / TIDEMARKED: “Stone Below the Water”

**Use:** Hall of families, underwater memorial, nursery, safe settlement discovered after descending into a flooded ruin.  
**Draft musical direction:** ~46 BPM, spacious 3/4, E minor / suspended harmonies. Three registers of voices enter in overlapping lines, female/male voices mixed. Low stone-like bass resonance with faint shell strikes; little percussion. For underwater hearing, use plausible filtered resonant audio rather than a giant dry cathedral reverb.

**Verse I**
> Below the reed, below the rain,  
> We keep the hall, we keep the flame.  
> The river takes the stars apart;  
> We gather each with both our hearts.

**Refrain**
> Sleep, little current, turn your wheel,  
> Carry the names the stones conceal.  
> Though every tower bends and falls,  
> The tide remembers all our halls.

**Verse II**
> The young ones learn the lantern's glow,  
> The older ones the depths below.  
> We lay our hands against the stone,  
> So none who wanders sings alone.

**Refrain**
> Sleep, little current, turn your wheel,  
> Carry the names the stones conceal.  
> Though every tower bends and falls,  
> The tide remembers all our halls.

**Bridge**
> If winter seals the upper stream,  
> We'll light the roots beneath the green.  
> When springs unbind the river's door,  
> We'll send our songs to shore once more.

**Outro**
> Carry the names. Carry them home.  
> Here in the deep, no one sings alone.

**Performance direction:** Tender, ancestral, not seduction. A community singing for its own people, not for the player. A nearby singer's breath should survive the resonant space so it never becomes a generic synth pad.

## Song 03 — SIRENS / LURESINGERS: “The Third Bell”

**Use:** Extremely rare still-water encounter, moonlit drowned causeway, or a distant song with no clearly visible singer.  
**Draft musical direction:** ~38 BPM implied; otherwise freely paced. F# Phrygian flavor, a recurring half-step rub, a single uncomfortably intimate voice answered from the wrong direction, thin second harmonics and an unresolved final note. Keep vocals understandable close by and uncertain in the distance. Some songs may be warnings, not traps.

**Verse I**
> One bell for the boats that sleep,  
> Two for the roads beneath the deep.  
> Three for the name you never said,  
> Hanging where the stars have fled.

**Refrain**
> Come no closer, turn no more,  
> You have heard this voice before.  
> Is it yours or is it mine?  
> Leave your lantern on the line.

**Verse II**
> I have kept a borrowed tune  
> From a mother and a moon.  
> If you hear it from the shore,  
> Ask whose mouth it wandered for.

**Refrain**
> Come no closer, turn no more,  
> You have heard this voice before.  
> Is it yours or is it mine?  
> Leave your lantern on the line.

**Bridge (without pulse; one voice almost speaking)**
> I won't promise home or harm.  
> I have neither net nor arm.  
> Only water, only air,  
> Only what you carried there.

**Closing line (unaccompanied)**
> I never called you by your name.  
> But still you turned. And still you came.

**Performance direction:** Unnerving, not constant threat or obvious coercion. The singer may be protecting the listener, grieving, or merely echoing something ancient. The same lyric can change meaning with encounter context.

## Soundscape and in-world behavior

| Situation | Proposed audio response |
| :-- | :-- |
| Approaching a Brinefolk settlement in clear weather | An identifiable call-and-response refrain carries over water before docks become visible |
| Nightfall or fog thickening | Village singing thins to shorter fragments, with fog-bells and water sounds more prominent |
| Passing a merfolk ruin | Low, filtered harmony may be perceived through stone and water; stronger near air chambers |
| Siren encounter | A remote solo line appears; reflections, occlusion and subtly displaced answering voice create spatial uncertainty |
| During a storm | Work songs stop or become urgent, and NPCs react to shelter needs rather than continuing an endless performance |
| Respectful visit or player trust | Optional variation in what the locals sing or how close the player can listen; no magical compulsion assumed |

**Design principle:** Songs should sometimes finish, stop unexpectedly or be replaced by mundane silence. Continuous looping destroys their power.

## Implementation notes (future, not current behavior)

1. Prototype with **one authored song event** and 2–3 locally played audio layers (lead, neighbors, natural sound). Set tempo/performance from content, not random global playback-rate changes.
2. Trigger as a **server-owned event ID and approximate start time** for cooperative consistency, while clients play local licensed audio assets with spatial attenuation, occlusion and volume settings. Do not stream audio over replication.
3. Split assets into solo, group and location-based stems so the mix responds to the player's distance without abruptly restarting a phrase.
4. Use contextual, budgeted NPC animation (mouth/body/cadence) and stop/transition cues rather than attempting hundreds of independently simulated vocalists.
5. Include **subtitles or lyric captions, directional indicators when relevant, separate music/voices volume controls, a way to disable siren-related disorientation**, and no essential navigation clues available by sound alone.
6. Test intelligibility on speakers/headphones, overlapping wildlife/wind, two clients entering at different times, server join-in-progress, and volume adjustments. Check rights for singers, performances, sound libraries and recordings before committing audio publicly.

## Small first recording and evaluation

Make 15–30 second *recorded* excerpts of each song with dry single-voice takes and one environmental bed. Test if players hear a village first, distinguish the cultures without visuals, and find the siren captivating but not irritating. Full compositions, finalized arrangements, voice casting, multilingual variants, dynamic NPC singing and advanced sound propagation are later decisions.

### Audible mockup limitations

A procedural demonstration may illustrate the three *musical palettes* with synthesized vowel-like tones, wooden knocks, bells and water ambience. Such a demonstration **is not a sung vocal performance of these lyrics**, a final soundtrack, an actual actor recording or an integrated UE5 feature.
