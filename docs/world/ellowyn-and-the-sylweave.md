# Wildbound — Ellowyn and the Sylweave

**Status:** **Confirmed owner lore premises** mixed with **exploratory story details**; this is an authored design document, not implemented narrative gameplay.  
**Updated:** October 21, 2026.

## What the owner has established

1. **Ellowyn** is the oldest sentient living tree, a colossal willow whose roots nourish the world.
2. **The Sylweave** is the ancient life force flowing through Ellowyn and woven through the entire realm. It **creates and connects every living creature**, including those that are cruel, predatory, malicious, or otherwise considered “bad.” The Sylweave is **not morally good or evil** and is not a corruption, alignment system, or reward for virtue. No class of living creatures is excluded from it.
3. A faction of creatures seeks to **mine the Sylweave for its own use**, especially youth and immortality, and players must stop them and save Ellowyn.
4. The extraction machines use a **specific chemical**. This artificial reagent contaminates Ellowyn while the machines siphon the Sylweave, **plaguing** her roots and spreading harm.
5. Over time **the same chemical poisons the creatures conducting the siphoning**. What was intended to make them young and immortal instead increasingly **transforms them into monsters**. The group is causing its own undoing.
6. **Because the plague contaminates their harvested batches**, the siphoners continue taking more Sylweave in hopes of finding a batch that has escaped the sickness. **Each additional attempt makes Ellowyn sicker and brings her closer to death.** The search for uncontaminated life force is itself perpetuating the contamination.
7. **With what strength she has left, Ellowyn deliberately shares her power with the player characters** so they can become heroes capable of rescuing her. This is the reason they are empowered, but not an instant grant of mastery: they must live off the land, understand living systems and the peoples of the realm, and grow stronger before they can stop the extraction and heal her.

The paragraphs below expand these clear owner decisions into *candidate* names, mechanics, visuals, encounters and plot beats; those details are not owner-approved canon yet.

## The Sylweave — nature without moral judgment

The Sylweave is a living, world-spanning weave of creative energy traveling from Ellowyn's roots into land, waters, and living bodies. It connects wild animals, farms, fantasy creatures, townsfolk, monsters and the faction exploiting it. It nourishes life and renewal but **does not decide who deserves to live**. Predation, dangerous creatures and morally wicked people are still part of the realm's living tapestry.

This produces a strong thematic contradiction: the faction siphoning Ellowyn **owes its own existence to the force it is destroying**. They cannot claim a right to the Sylweave on the grounds that they are more worthy of life.

The Sylweave is *not* equivalent to the blight: one connects and sustains life, the other is an artificial chemical contaminant. Ellowyn's weakness results from a combination of deliberate extraction and poisoning; this difference should remain consistent in dialogue, visual effects and gameplay design.

## Ellowyn's last gift — why the player becomes a hero

**Owner-confirmed central premise:** Ellowyn has been steadily drained and poisoned. She can no longer sever the far-flung extraction lines herself. In a conscious act of desperation and trust, she spends whatever strength she can still spare and bestows a **personal thread of her Sylweave** upon the player characters. This is a **special intentional gift**, not a claim that players alone possess the Sylweave: **every creature, including the enemy, is naturally connected to it already**. Ellowyn does not withhold life itself from those she considers wicked.

**Narrative consequence:** The gift leaves her even weaker and makes the rescue deeply personal. What she shares is a **seed of possibility, not a completed heroic skill set**. A newly arrived settler has the potential to grow into the hero she needs, but not yet the understanding, strength, courage or training.

**Thematic rule:** There is no automatic chosen bloodline or requirement to be born uniquely worthy. Ellowyn actively chooses to place hope in people who can move where her roots cannot and may learn to listen to the world. In cooperative play, **each player can share equally in the story's calling**, with no designated 'real hero' who owns the progression.

**A possible first meeting (original draft, not finalized dialogue):**

> “Little wanderer, there is so little of me left to give. Take it. Carry it where my roots can no longer reach. But do not mistake a gift for understanding. Learn the land. Know its creatures. Live among those I have sheltered. Then perhaps... you may teach my heart to beat freely again.”

### Learn the land, grow the gift

As players settle and survive, Ellowyn's gift develops through **earned experience of the living world**, rather than exclusively by killing enemies or accumulating generic experience points.

| Player experience | The lesson | Illustrative gift progression — not an implemented mechanic |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Construct shelter; maintain clean water, gardens and soil | Life needs a home and room to change | Detect a faint Sylweave pulse near a creek or damaged grove |
| Observe, tame through trust, care for and responsibly breed animals | A living being cannot be treated as a disposable resource | Sense distress in nearby creatures or recognize a disrupted habitat |
| Explore regions, track migrations, weather and signs of blight | Each ecosystem expresses the Sylweave differently | Follow distant root currents, identify unseen extraction damage |
| Befriend and assist elven, wolfkin, dwarven, aquatic and original peoples | Cultures hold pieces of the world's history and practical knowledge | Recognize the symbols and stories used to hide Weir connections |
| Learn survival, craft equipment, face threats, cleanse small contaminated sites | Courage and technical ability must complement compassion | Resist dangerous siphon environments and help sever an extraction line |

**Power must complement, not replace, the core homesteading game.** It should never grant effortless taming, instant crop growth, or magical immunity to animal needs. Care, building, collaboration and earned trust remain meaningful even after story victory. The ability to fight and survive should grow, but the central goal is to **heal**, not simply to become powerful enough to destroy things.

**Story progression candidate:** the players first discover a single harmed root, are entrusted with the gift, learn to restore one tiny patch of land, then gradually build the competence and alliances needed to confront the Great Siphon. The pacing should permit an open-ended life on persistent servers rather than impose a punishing real-time doomsday clock.

### The moral paradox

The antagonists' lives also come from Ellowyn. She cannot casually remove them from the Sylweave without denying what she is. Her gift to the players is a request for help against their *actions*, not a decree that enemies are not worth living. This leaves room for infected enemies who might still be saved, and allows a dark but humane story.

## The chemical — working concept: Nhalis

**Nhalis** (working name; not owner-approved) is a manufactured binding reagent, infused or circulated through machines to separate and stabilize the living Sylweave into a transportable, concentrated product provisionally named **Heartglass** (also a working name).

The faction originally believed Nhalis made Sylweave safe to store and ingest. Early experiments appeared to reverse age, mend scars and sustain failing bodies. The apparent miracle drove increasingly large-scale extraction and reckless self-experimentation.

But Nhalis accumulates, reacts unpredictably with living tissues, and becomes increasingly harmful as the dose builds. It does **not** represent ordinary Sylweave biology and cannot be countered merely by “more pure energy.” The chemical is fictional; no real-world formulation, ingredients, synthesis or extraction procedure are proposed.

### The untainted batch that never comes — owner-established central plot mechanism

The siphoners keep searching for *pure*, unaffected Sylweave. But their machinery introduces **Nhalis** (working chemical name) during extraction, and repeated operation contaminates Ellowyn at the point of contact. She is not a bottomless collection of isolated reservoirs: the Sylweave **weaves through living roots and every creature in the realm**.

The story's central feedback loop is:

1. They draw Sylweave with machines that introduce the chemical.
2. The harvested batch tests **plagued/impure**, and some of the extractors or consumers begin to sicken.
3. Instead of stopping, they insist that a still-pure reservoir must exist farther away, deeper beneath the earth, or beyond a poisoned stretch of root.
4. They drill farther, run the siphons longer, and expose still more living root to the chemical.
5. Ellowyn weakens; even the new sites begin yielding contaminated Sylweave. The faction demands **another batch**.
6. This spiral threatens to kill Ellowyn, destroying the source of all Sylweave, and therefore the lives of the very people stealing it.

**Core irony:** Their attempts to harvest uncontaminated Sylweave are the reason they cannot obtain it. They interpret the chemical damage as a defect in the source rather than a consequence of their own method. A later discovery may prove this through a comparison between samples taken near an active siphon and naturally living, undisturbed Sylweave.

**Keep the distinction clear:** The plague is the unnatural chemical's injury and contamination, **not a moral taint within Sylweave**, and there is no class of creature inherently excluded from its living weave. Animals, peaceful villages, predatory creatures, and the harmful faction all depend on Ellowyn. This gives the protagonists a strong reason to stop the chemical exposure and the siphoning, rather than purging a supposedly evil branch of life.

**Open dramatic choice:** Whether the senior leaders truly believe a clean batch lies deeper, know their method is the cause and conceal it, or disagree internally is not yet decided. The owner's specified relentless hunt for a pure batch and Ellowyn's consequent decline remain fixed.

### Ellowyn's sickness

- Near the extraction sites, her roots grow brittle and develop pale crystalline lesions under a layer of dark, wet-looking blight.
- The contamination travels through connected root systems and leaks into isolated soils and waterways. It need not plague every animal or village automatically: local impacts should follow exposure and ecological pathways.
- Leaf canopies yellow or fall out of season; once-reliable springs turn still; flowers bloom but produce no seed. Ellowyn can feel the damage but may be unable to trace every source.
- Her ancient will and personality remain; she is not a passive reactor or a villain becoming “evil” because she is sick.
- Treat the contamination visually as poisoning and injury. Avoid making her all-encompassing magic effortlessly cure it; removing the cause and giving her time to heal matters.

### The siphoners' reversal of immortality

| Stage | What the faction believes | What is actually happening | Story / visual cues |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **I — False Renewal** | An apparent cure for age and frailty | Nhalis begins to accumulate, even as concentrated Sylweave produces short-lived restorative effects | Youthful faces, reflective eyes, faint dark or pale veins, missing sleep |
| **II — The Blight** | Uncomfortable “adjustment” that more treatments will solve | Chemical injury drives asymmetrical bodily change and mounting dependency | Unnatural bone and crystalline protrusions, painful sensory changes, distorted voices, altered behavior |
| **III — The Threnvahl** | Leadership calls cases rare failures or suppresses reports | Profound mutation produces powerful, dangerous and sometimes confused monsters among the most heavily exposed | Former workers and leaders recognizable by clothes, keepsakes, tools, scraps of speech; some may retain memory or agency |

**The Threnvahl** is the owner-selected collective name for chemically transformed creatures across species, not a naturally occurring species. Mutation does not automatically erase a character's personhood or excuse faction crimes. Not everyone exposed must become aggressive, and a possible treatment or rescue route should remain a narrative choice. The source of the monstrosity is the faction's chemical exposure, not a pre-existing fantasy species, disability, or a “bad” alignment in the Sylweave.

### An unsettling revelation

The first laboratory looks prosperous: portraits of newly youthful patrons, a beautifully bound research book, polished vials said to promise eternal life. Beyond its sealed wards lie the failed subjects: the creators themselves, still breathing and barely recognizable, some whispering that the machines must not be restarted.

One possible dramatic reading is that certain leaders know each deeper siphon worsens the sickness and hide the evidence, while others still cling to the hope of a clean batch. Alternatively, the leadership might be sincerely mistaken until the protagonists expose the extraction feedback loop. The faction continues pursuing uncontaminated Sylweave, and Ellowyn pays for every failed attempt. The chemical blights **both victim and perpetrator**, but Ellowyn never chose to be used.

## Villains and stakes (working names)

**The Hollow Crown** (proposed name) is a coalition of patrons, engineers, miners, and creatures pursuing Sylweave-derived longevity and power. Distinguish leaders who knowingly keep the scheme running from coerced workers, infected members and outsiders. The Hollow Crown need not be a single species.

The proposed **Gilded Warden** believes that by controlling the Sylweave and perfecting Nhalis, he can save his followers from death. As his own transformation develops, he may become physically monstrous without admitting the experiment has failed.

**Heartglass** remains the draft name for the refined commodity; **Nhalis** is the **toxic chemical reagent** used to harvest and stabilize it; **the Sylweave** is the **natural life force**. Do not confuse or merge these three.

## How this changes the main story

### Chapter I — Ellowyn's Last Gift
A homesteader discovers a dying root near a creek, strange livestock or wildlife behavior, and traces of pale industrial sediment. Ellowyn, nearly spent, reaches the player characters and invests what strength she can spare in them. This moment **makes them potential heroes but not yet competent saviors**. A damaged animal may be helped, highlighting the player's caring role. The act ends with a discovery: this is not a spontaneous curse; it traces back to a machine and someone operating it.

### Chapter II — The Price of Years
**Players must first live on the land and grow stronger.** Building and maintaining a homestead, learning wildlife needs, earning animal trust, survival, crafting and exploring varied habitats develop the understanding necessary to use Ellowyn's gift. Investigating different settlements uncovers claims of miraculous rejuvenation, villages dependent on Heartglass, hidden waste from refinement, and the first visibly altered siphoners. Dwarven experts may recognize ancient Weir architecture, fungal keepers can detect disrupted growth, and werewolf trackers may find new routes taken by frightened animals. Players learn why some factions cooperate and others resist.

### Chapter III — Drowned Evidence
The Drowned March holds records of the older Weir catastrophe and song-preserved routes to buried infrastructure. Players compare records of contaminated batches with the ever-deeper drilling pattern, revealing that the supposed search for a clean source is itself poisoning Ellowyn. The faction's handling of illness among its own members—denial, concealment, or genuine confusion—can become a separate moral discovery. Some Threnvahl retain enough awareness to reveal locations or ask for mercy. Sirens may sing warnings about waters no longer answering Ellowyn.

### Chapter IV — The Great Siphon
After living in and learning the world, the players are finally strong enough to carry Ellowyn's entrusted gift into the buried heart of the extraction system. The climax takes place in a chamber of enormous willow roots, old stone mechanisms and the faction's chemical distribution network. The principal antagonist may be the most altered of all, demonstrating the failure of their immortality project. Merely killing a mutated leader does **not** cure Ellowyn, and one supposedly pure batch is not the solution: players must stop the repeated extraction, interrupt the chemical feed, prevent or contain more contamination, and stabilize essential protections for villages still dependent on old Weirs. The use of Ellowyn's gift in the finale should **depend on lessons learned from animals, habitats, villages and survival**, not on raw magic levels alone.

**Ellowyn must remain an active participant in the ending**: once the extraction is interrupted and the contamination contained, she chooses how her strength returns to the world's life network. She does not purge her enemies from the Sylweave; they are living beings too.

## What counts as beating Wildbound?

**Story victory:** Grow into Ellowyn's final gift through lived experience, shut down the Sylweave siphoning enterprise, end the deliberate contamination, defeat or stop the leadership, protect dependent communities from sudden collapse, and give Ellowyn a credible path to recover.

The finale is emotionally and visually conclusive (boss/confrontation shape still open), followed by story credits and a visible improvement in Ellowyn and the land. Existing player homes, animals, villages and cooperative saves persist afterward; the story's victory is not the end of life in the world.

Recovery should be **hopeful rather than instant or perfectly clean**. Residual illness, contested research, repentant surviving Threnvahl and the care of recovering habitats can support post-story content. No forced purge, world wipe, or morally selective Sylweave is implied.

## Candidate terms — undecided, not canon

- **Nhalis** — the harmful manufactured chemical used in extraction and refinement.
- **Heartglass** — concentrate refined from stolen Sylweave.
- **The Threnvahl** — **owner-selected name** for chemically transformed beings; individual variants and abilities are undecided.
- **The Hollow Crown** — the exploiting faction.
- **The Gilded Warden** — a principal faction leader; his species, motivations, personality and finale remain unresolved.
- **The Weirs** — ancient regulating machinery now repurposed to extract Sylweave.

**These names are optional creative directions.** Sylweave is explicitly selected as the life-force name; Ellowyn's act of gifting her remaining power to players, and their need to learn the land before they can save her, are also owner-confirmed story premises.

## Technical / ownership boundaries

Everything in this file is a lore proposal apart from the owner-confirmed creative premises at top. The Unreal prototype does not contain a living Ellowyn character, player Sylweave gift or progression system, mining machines, chemical poisoning, mutated characters, faction AI, narrative missions or an endgame. The documented Greenveil build and listen-server connection checks are separate evidence; none of these features has been compiled, implemented or playtested.
