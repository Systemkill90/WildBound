# Wildbound — Ellowyn and the Sylweave

**Updated:** October 21, 2026.

## What the owner has established

1. **Ellowyn** is the oldest sentient living tree, a colossal willow whose roots nourish the world.
2. **The Sylweave** is the ancient life force flowing through Ellowyn and woven through the entire realm. It **creates and connects every living creature**, including those that are cruel, predatory, malicious, or otherwise considered “bad.” The Sylweave is **not morally good or evil** and is not a corruption, alignment system, or reward for virtue. No class of living creatures is excluded from it.
3. A faction of creatures seeks to **mine the Sylweave for its own use**, especially youth and immortality, and players must stop them and save Ellowyn.
4. The extraction machines use a **specific chemical**. This artificial reagent contaminates Ellowyn while the machines siphon the Sylweave, **plaguing** her roots and spreading harm.
5. Over time **the same chemical poisons the creatures conducting the siphoning**. What was intended to make them young and immortal instead increasingly **transforms them into monsters**. The group is causing its own undoing.

The paragraphs below expand these clear owner decisions into *candidate* names, mechanics, visuals, encounters and plot beats; those details are not owner-approved canon yet.

## The Sylweave — nature without moral judgment

The Sylweave is a living, world-spanning weave of creative energy traveling from Ellowyn's roots into land, waters, and living bodies. It connects wild animals, farms, fantasy creatures, townsfolk, monsters and the faction exploiting it. It nourishes life and renewal but **does not decide who deserves to live**. Predation, dangerous creatures and morally wicked people are still part of the realm's living tapestry.

This produces a strong thematic contradiction: the faction siphoning Ellowyn **owes its own existence to the force it is destroying**. They cannot claim a right to the Sylweave on the grounds that they are more worthy of life.

The Sylweave is *not* equivalent to the blight: one connects and sustains life, the other is an artificial chemical contaminant. Ellowyn's weakness results from a combination of deliberate extraction and poisoning; this difference should remain consistent in dialogue, visual effects and gameplay design.

## The chemical — working concept: Nhalis

**Nhalis** (working name; not owner-approved) is a manufactured binding reagent, infused or circulated through machines to separate and stabilize the living Sylweave into a transportable, concentrated product provisionally named **Heartglass** (also a working name).

The faction originally believed Nhalis made Sylweave safe to store and ingest. Early experiments appeared to reverse age, mend scars and sustain failing bodies. The apparent miracle drove increasingly large-scale extraction and reckless self-experimentation.

But Nhalis accumulates, reacts unpredictably with living tissues, and becomes increasingly harmful as the dose builds. It does **not** represent ordinary Sylweave biology and cannot be countered merely by “more pure energy.” The chemical is fictional; no real-world formulation, ingredients, synthesis or extraction procedure are proposed.

### Ellowyn's sickness

- Near the extraction sites, her roots grow brittle and develop pale crystalline lesions under a layer of dark, wet-looking blight.
- The contamination travels through connected root systems and leaks into isolated soils and waterways. It need not plague every animal or village automatically: local impacts should follow exposure and ecological pathways.
- Leaf canopies yellow or fall out of season; once-reliable springs turn still; flowers bloom but produce no seed. Ellowyn can feel the damage but may be unable to trace every source.
- Her ancient will and personality remain; she is not a passive reactor or a villain becoming “evil” because she is sick.
- Treat the contamination visually as poisoning and injury. Avoid making her all-encompassing magic effortlessly cure it; removing the cause and giving her time to heal matters.

### The siphoners' reversal of immortality

| Stage | What the faction believes | What is actually happening | Story / visual cues |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **I — False Renewal** | An apparent cure for age and frailty | Nhalis begins to accumulate, even as concentrated Sylweave produces short-lived restorative effects | Youthful faces, reflective eyes, faint dark or pale veins, missing sleep |
| **II — The Blight** | Uncomfortable “adjustment” that more treatments will solve | Chemical injury drives asymmetrical bodily change and mounting dependency | Unnatural bone and crystalline protrusions, painful sensory changes, distorted voices, altered behavior |
| **III — The Hollowed** | Leadership calls cases rare failures or suppresses reports | Profound mutation produces powerful, dangerous and sometimes confused monsters among the most heavily exposed | Former workers and leaders recognizable by clothes, keepsakes, tools, scraps of speech; some may retain memory or agency |

**The Hollowed** is a proposed collective epithet, not a finalized species name. Mutation does not automatically erase a character's personhood or excuse faction crimes. Not everyone exposed must become aggressive, and a possible treatment or rescue route should remain a narrative choice. The source of the monstrosity is the faction's chemical exposure, not a pre-existing fantasy species, disability, or a “bad” alignment in the Sylweave.

### An unsettling revelation

The first laboratory looks prosperous: portraits of newly youthful patrons, a beautifully bound research book, polished vials said to promise eternal life. Beyond its sealed wards lie the failed subjects: the creators themselves, still breathing and barely recognizable, some whispering that the machines must not be restarted.

The faction leaders knew that every round of refinement worsened the sickness. Some hide the evidence; some double down because they fear aging or death; others want to stop but cannot escape their own employers. The chemical blights **both victim and perpetrator**, but Ellowyn never chose to be used.

## Villains and stakes (working names)

**The Hollow Crown** (proposed name) is a coalition of patrons, engineers, miners, and creatures pursuing Sylweave-derived longevity and power. Distinguish leaders who knowingly keep the scheme running from coerced workers, infected members and outsiders. The Hollow Crown need not be a single species.

The proposed **Gilded Warden** believes that by controlling the Sylweave and perfecting Nhalis, he can save his followers from death. As his own transformation develops, he may become physically monstrous without admitting the experiment has failed.

**Heartglass** remains the draft name for the refined commodity; **Nhalis** is the **toxic chemical reagent** used to harvest and stabilize it; **the Sylweave** is the **natural life force**. Do not confuse or merge these three.

## How this changes the main story

### Chapter I — Something Beneath the Meadow
A homesteader discovers a dying root near a creek, strange livestock or wildlife behavior, and traces of pale industrial sediment. A damaged animal may be helped, highlighting the player's caring role. The first act ends with a discovery: this is not a mystical curse spreading spontaneously; it traces back to a machine and someone operating it.

### Chapter II — The Price of Years
Investigating different settlements uncovers claims of miraculous rejuvenation, villages dependent on Heartglass, hidden waste from refinement, and the first visibly altered siphoners. Dwarven experts may recognize ancient Weir architecture, fungal keepers can detect disrupted growth, and werewolf trackers may find new routes taken by frightened animals. Players learn why some factions cooperate and others resist.

### Chapter III — Drowned Evidence
The Drowned March holds records of the older Weir catastrophe and song-preserved routes to buried infrastructure. Players discover that the faction's leaders have concealed the illness among their own members. Some Hollowed retain enough awareness to reveal locations or ask for mercy. Sirens may sing warnings about waters no longer answering Ellowyn.

### Chapter IV — The Great Siphon
The climax takes place in a chamber of enormous willow roots, old stone mechanisms and the faction's chemical distribution network. The principal antagonist may be the most altered of all, demonstrating the failure of their immortality project. Merely killing a mutated leader does **not** cure Ellowyn: players must stop the siphon, prevent or contain more chemical release, and stabilize essential protections for villages still dependent on old Weirs.

**Ellowyn must remain an active participant in the ending**: once the extraction is interrupted and the contamination contained, she chooses how her strength returns to the world's life network. She does not purge her enemies from the Sylweave; they are living beings too.

## What counts as beating Wildbound?

**Story victory:** Shut down the Sylweave siphoning enterprise, end the deliberate contamination, defeat or stop the leadership, protect dependent communities from sudden collapse, and give Ellowyn a credible path to recover.

The finale is emotionally and visually conclusive (boss/confrontation shape still open), followed by story credits and a visible improvement in Ellowyn and the land. Existing player homes, animals, villages and cooperative saves persist afterward; the story's victory is not the end of life in the world.

Recovery should be **hopeful rather than instant or perfectly clean**. Residual illness, contested research, repentant surviving Hollowed and the care of recovering habitats can support post-story content. No forced purge, world wipe, or morally selective Sylweave is implied.

## Candidate terms — undecided, not canon

- **Nhalis** — the harmful manufactured chemical used in extraction and refinement.
- **Heartglass** — concentrate refined from stolen Sylweave.
- **The Hollowed** — transformed members and victims of the chemical.
- **The Hollow Crown** — the exploiting faction.
- **The Gilded Warden** — a principal faction leader; his species, motivations, personality and finale remain unresolved.
- **The Weirs** — ancient regulating machinery now repurposed to extract Sylweave.

**These names are optional creative directions; only Sylweave is explicitly selected as the life-force name.**
